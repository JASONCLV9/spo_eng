\begin{abstract}
    \noindent A recorded left-handed table tennis topspin stroke (Xsens MVN, \SI{60}{\hertz}; inverse kinematics in OpenSim) is extended into a Vizard~8.3.1 desktop scene to examine a recorded-path feasibility question: whether this specific recorded racket sweep geometrically intercepts a static simulated ball positioned at an outward offset $d$ from the peak-speed impact point. Fidelity is evaluated against OpenSim at three timestamps ($t = 2.500$, $3.083$, $3.600$~s). Elbow flexion differs by $+3.57^\circ$, $+5.12^\circ$ and $-0.09^\circ$; hand height differs by $-0.039$, $+0.027$ and $+0.104$~m. Four ordered offsets ($0.00$, $0.08$, $0.15$, $0.30$~m) straddle a predicted boundary $\Rc = 0.095$~m: the former two satisfy the geometric criterion, whereas the latter two do not. All results are \tagsim; as no participant judgments were collected, the judgment protocol remains \tagprop. Camera viewpoint is analysed as a determinant of the available optical information (foreshortening, occlusion, flow).
\end{abstract}
