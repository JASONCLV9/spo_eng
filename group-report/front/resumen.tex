\begin{abstract}
    This report extends an experimental table tennis movement (a left-handed topspin stroke captured with \Xsens{} and biomechanically modeled in \OpenSim{}) into an interactive, reproducible virtual reality scene in \Vizard{} (v8.3.1) to examine an actor-relative action possibility (\emph{affordance}) under James J. Gibson's ecological framework. We detail the kinematic coordinate transformation between systems and validate movement fidelity by comparing \OpenSim{} and \Vizard{} at three representative timestamps ($t = 2.500\text{ s}$, $3.083\text{ s}$, and $3.600\text{ s}$). Numerical verification demonstrates excellent agreement across both angular kinematics (left elbow flexion discrepancy $\le 5.12^{\circ}$) and spatial positioning (hand height discrepancy $< 0.11\text{ m}$).

    The affordance investigation assesses the recorded-path feasibility of geometric interception between the sweeping racket and a simulated ball across four ordered environmental offset conditions ($d \in \{0.00, 0.08, 0.15, 0.30\}\text{ m}$), crossing a predicted physical contact boundary of $0.095\text{ m}$. Results confirm successful interception for conditions 1 and 2 ($0.000\text{ m}$ and $0.080\text{ m}$), and failure to intercept for conditions 3 and 4 ($0.150\text{ m}$ and $0.296\text{ m}$). We critically address the distinction between affordance, perceptual judgment, and athletic performance outcome, analyzing ambient optical flow invariants, touch--posture covariation, and the physical limitations of a desktop visual simulation devoid of dynamic haptic feedback.

    \vspace{0.2cm}
    \noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Affordance, James J. Gibson, Table Tennis, Vizard, OpenSim, Xsens MVN, Geometric Interception, Sports Biomechanics.
\end{abstract}
