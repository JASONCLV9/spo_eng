\begin{abstract}
\noindent We extend a recorded left-handed table tennis topspin stroke (Xsens MVN, \SI{60}{\hertz}; inverse kinematics in OpenSim) into a Vizard~8.3.1 desktop scene and ask a \textbf{recorded-path feasibility question}: does this specific recorded racket sweep geometrically intercept a static simulated ball placed at an outward offset $d$ from the peak-speed impact point? Fidelity is checked against OpenSim at three timestamps ($t = 2.500$, $3.083$, $3.600$~s). Elbow flexion differs by $+3.57^\circ$, $+5.12^\circ$ and $-0.09^\circ$; hand height by $-0.039$, $+0.027$ and $+0.104$~m. Four ordered offsets ($0.00$, $0.08$, $0.15$, $0.30$~m) straddle a predicted boundary $\Rc = 0.095$~m: the first two satisfy the geometric criterion, the last two do not. All results are \tagsim; no participant judgments were collected, so the judgment protocol is \tagprop. Camera viewpoint is analysed as a determinant of the optical information available (foreshortening, occlusion, flow), not of the affordance itself. The scene provides visual information only, not haptic interaction.
\end{abstract}
